# Research Index
## Psychology Frameworks Mapped to Chapters

This file maps each chapter to the relevant psychological research, terms, and frameworks that explain the behavior documented in the source material.

## Core Frameworks

### Gottman's Four Horsemen (John Gottman)
The four communication patterns most predictive of relationship failure:
1. **Criticism** — attacking character rather than behavior
2. **Contempt** — superiority, mockery, eye-rolling (the single strongest predictor of divorce)
3. **Defensiveness** — deflecting accountability, counter-attacking
4. **Stonewalling** — shutting down, withdrawing, refusing to engage

*Chapters: 3, 6, 7, 8, 16*

**Key sources:**
- Gottman, J.M. & Silver, N. (1999). *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work.*
- Gottman, J.M. (1994). *What Predicts Divorce: The Relationship Between Marital Processes and Marital Outcomes.*

---

### DARVO (Jennifer Freyd)
**Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender** — a pattern where the person being held accountable denies the behavior, attacks the person raising it, and positions themselves as the real victim.

*Chapters: 5, 6, 7, 8, 12*

**Key sources:**
- Freyd, J.J. (1997). *Violations of power, adaptive blindness, and betrayal trauma theory.*
- Harsey, S.J., Zurbriggen, E.L., & Freyd, J.J. (2017). *Perpetrator responses to victim confrontation: DARVO and victim self-blame.*

---

### Emotional Invalidation
The dismissal, minimization, or denial of another person's emotional experience. Can be overt ("you're overreacting") or structural (ending conversations before feelings are acknowledged).

*Chapters: 3, 6, 8, 13, 16*

**Key sources:**
- Linehan, M.M. (1993). *Cognitive-Behavioral Treatment of Borderline Personality Disorder.*
- Shenk, C.E. & Fruzzetti, A.E. (2011). *The impact of validating and invalidating responses on emotional reactivity.*

---

### Gaslighting (Parrott, Sweet, Abramson)
A form of psychological manipulation in which a person causes another to question their own memory, perception, or sanity. The term originates from the 1944 film *Gaslight*. Modern research distinguishes ambient gaslighting (cumulative, low-level) from acute episodes.

*Chapter: 5, 8*

**Key sources:**
- Sweet, P.L. (2019). *The sociology of gaslighting.* American Sociological Review.
- Abramson, K. (2014). *Turning up the lights on gaslighting.* Philosophical Perspectives.

---

### Unilateral Decision-Making and Relationship Power Imbalance
Research on how asymmetric decision-making authority erodes perceived partnership, trust, and belonging in intimate relationships.

*Chapters: 1, 2, 4*

**Key sources:**
- Kolb, D. & Williams, J. (2003). *Everyday Negotiation.*
- Blood, R.O. & Wolfe, D.M. (1960). *Husbands and Wives: The Dynamics of Married Living.* (foundational; critiqued and updated by feminist scholars)
- Avellar, S. & Smock, P. (2005). *The economic consequences of the dissolution of cohabiting unions.*

---

### The Sick Role (Talcott Parsons, updated)
Parsons' original formulation of the "sick role" as a social position with defined rights (exemption from normal duties) and obligations (seeking to get well). Later researchers examined how the sick role can be instrumentalized — consciously or not — within intimate relationships.

*Chapter: 9*

**Key sources:**
- Parsons, T. (1951). *The Social System.*
- Williams, S.J. (2005). *Parsons revisited: From the sick role to...?* Health.
- Charmaz, K. (1995). *The body, identity, and self: Adapting to impairment.*

---

### Positive Reinforcement and Relationship Maintenance (Skinner → Gottman)
Behavioral research on how the ratio of positive to negative interactions predicts relationship stability. Gottman's research found a 5:1 positive-to-negative ratio in stable relationships; in relationships heading for dissolution, this ratio inverts.

*Chapters: 13, 14, 15*

**Key sources:**
- Gottman, J.M. & Levenson, R.W. (1992). *Marital processes predictive of later dissolution.*
- Skinner, B.F. (1938). *The Behavior of Organisms.* (foundational operant conditioning)

---

### Attachment Theory and Protest Behavior (Bowlby, Mikulincer, Shaver)
Bowlby's attachment theory and its application to adult relationships. When attachment needs are unmet, individuals engage in "protest behavior" — escalating emotional expression to re-establish connection. In dismissing attachment systems, protest behavior intensifies and can appear as anger, demand, or withdrawal.

*Chapters: 13, 17*

**Key sources:**
- Bowlby, J. (1969). *Attachment and Loss, Vol. 1.*
- Mikulincer, M. & Shaver, P. (2016). *Attachment in Adulthood: Structure, Dynamics, and Change.*
- Johnson, S.M. (2008). *Hold Me Tight.*

---

### Stonewalling and Physiological Flooding (Gottman)
When one partner's heart rate exceeds ~100bpm during conflict, the prefrontal cortex (responsible for reasoning and empathy) becomes significantly impaired. Stonewalling often begins as a self-protective response to flooding — but is experienced by the other partner as contempt or rejection.

*Chapter: 3*

**Key sources:**
- Gottman, J.M. & Levenson, R.W. (1988). *The social psychophysiology of marriage.*

---

### Self-Serving Attributional Bias
The tendency to attribute one's own successes to internal factors and failures to external factors — while doing the reverse for others. In relationship conflict, this manifests as "my actions were circumstantial; your actions reflect who you are."

*Chapters: 6, 7, 8*

**Key sources:**
- Miller, D.T. & Ross, M. (1975). *Self-serving biases in attribution of causality.*
- Bradbury, T.N. & Fincham, F.D. (1990). *Attributions in marriage: Review and critique.*

---

### The Power of Acknowledgment (Heen & Stone)
Research on the specific, measurable impact of being acknowledged — versus being praised — in close relationships. Acknowledgment requires naming what the other person did and that you noticed it. Praise does not.

*Chapters: 13, 14, 15*

**Key sources:**
- Stone, D. & Heen, S. (2014). *Thanks for the Feedback.*
- Harter, S. (1999). *The Construction of the Self.*

---

### Love Languages and Unmet Attachment Needs (Chapman)
Gary Chapman's framework identifying five primary ways people give and receive love: words of affirmation, acts of service, receiving gifts, quality time, and physical touch. Mismatched love languages create systematic unmet needs even in relationships where both people are genuinely trying.

*Chapters: 13, 14, 18*

**Key sources:**
- Chapman, G. (1992). *The Five Love Languages.*
- Mostova, O., Stolarski, M., & Matthews, G. (2022). *I love the way you love me: Responding to partner's love language preferences boosts satisfaction.*
