# Chapter 12: I Will Never Need a Man
## *How to declare independence during a moment of dependence*

---

> *"I will go on record – I don't expect you to put food on the table. I was raised to do it for myself and my family. I will never need a man for it."*
> — Priya, January 25, 2025

---

## The Method

The most vulnerable state a human being can enter is dependency. When we are sick, financially exposed, or trying to raise an infant, we *must* rely on others. This reliance is the raw material of intimacy; it is the bridge of trust that allows two people to become a single unit.

To destroy a partnership, you simply need to accept all the practical benefits of your partner's backup, while explicitly and aggressively declaring that you do not need them at all.

This is the method of "I Will Never Need a Man." It is a highly effective defensive buffer because it prevents your partner's support from ever converting into structural power or emotional currency in the marriage.

This method operates through three distinct maneuvers:

1. **The Asymmetric Standard:** You demand absolute, unconditional support from your partner (such as their full paycheck flowing into joint accounts, their extraction of space for your family, and their daily caregiving backup) while reserving the right to consolidate absolute, unilateral control over all major assets.
2. **The Sovereign Declaration:** Declaring your complete romantic, physical, and financial independence in the heat of a domestic argument—framing your independence not as a private strength, but as a public insulation against your partner's "control."
3. **The Dependency Tax:** Treating their financial or physical support of your household as a low-level, routine obligation they are *expected* to perform, while framing your occasional support of them as a massive, generous favor that demands their undying gratitude.

Once this structure is active, your partner is caught in a exhausting financial and emotional bypass. They are funding an empire in which they are not allowed to be a citizen, and contributing their life’s energy to a partner who declares, on the record, that their contribution has zero value.

## In Practice

The text record of Marcus and Priya's marital finances and spatial arrangements showcases this pattern of unrequited, aggressive independence. 

By early 2025, Marcus’s financial and spatial standing had been completely hollowed out (documented in [chapters/01-the-unilateral-decision.md](chapters/01-the-unilateral-decision.md) and [chapters/07-maturity-is-needed.md](chapters/07-maturity-is-needed.md)). He had no independent credit cards, no independent access to their joint family trust accounts, and had to ask Priya’s permission to pay his own therapist using his HSA card. 

His paycheck, however, flowed consistently into accounts he did not control. He was structurally dependent.

Yet during arguments about how this structure was operating, Priya consistently reframed his financial dependency as a personal favor she was granting him, while simultaneously declaring her absolute financial sovereignty.

On January 25, 2025, during an argument about domestic tasks and financial contributions, Priya delivered a formal, sharp declaration of independence:

> **Priya (Jan 25, 2025):** *"I will go on record – I don't expect you to put food on the table. I was raised to do it for myself and my family. I will never need a man for it."*

The timing of this message is worth noting. Priya was, at this exact moment, suffering from severe chronic fatigue (ME/CFS) and was physically dependent on Marcus for their son’s daily care, grocery runs, and bedtime routines (documented in [chapters/09-the-sick-role.md](chapters/09-the-sick-role.md)). She was also occupying a million-dollar home co-funded by Marcus's income. 

Yet by declaring, *"I will never need a man for it,"* she retroactively insulated herself from the vulnerability of her dependency. She accepted the food Marcus put on the table, but declared that she didn't *expect* it, thereby stripping the act of any relational significance.

---

The declaration of independence was not a rare, defensive utterance; it was a consistent rhetorical posture. It extended from financial self-sufficiency to her personal relationships. 

In March 2025, Priya made unilateral arrangements for their household, moving her friend and her parents permanently into the shared home without consulting Marcus. When Marcus protested that his spatial privacy was being violated, Priya did not negotiate. Instead, she informed Marcus that his opinions on her personal relationships were completely unwelcome, and that she would gladly replace his role as a husband with her own family network:

> **Priya (Feb 6, 2024):** *"I will do whatever to manage our son and no one has any right on where, when and how I do it... I will manage with my parents till I can."*

And a year later:

> **Priya (March 18, 2025):** *"I have no expectations from you including in my old age... I am on my own."*

The message is consistent: *I do not need you. I do not expect anything from you. My parents, my friends, and my career are my true support system. Your participation is optional, and your objections are irrelevant.*

---

This posture of complete self-sufficiency was used to justify the unilateral consolidation of financial assets. 

In late 2023, while Marcus’s financial authority was being systematically reduced, Priya announced a unilateral consolidation of all family bookings, cards, and reservation authority:

> **Priya (Nov 19, 2023):** *"From now on I will be primary on all CC and reservations."*

And when Marcus raised concerns about his complete lack of financial visibility, Priya reframed his structural dependency as a personal convenience she was funding out of the goodness of her heart, demanding his gratitude for her paying "his bills":

> **Priya (March 10, 2025):** *"Why can't you even do that when I pay your medical bills and all kinds of bills everyday."*
> **Priya (May 16, 2025):** *"I owe and get nothing in this process and I am going out of way to pay your bills."*

Marcus's income was part of the collective pool, but because Priya managed the transactions, she recast her administrative execution as personal charity. She was the sovereign provider; Marcus was a dependent child who should be grateful he was being "allowed" to have his bills paid.

---

This selective framing reached its structural peak in how Priya categorized shared familial needs. While Priya's corporate earnings were pooled to manage the joint trust properties, the basic caregiving support required to execute Marcus's primary care routines was unilaterally reclassified as his personal convenience. 

In January 2025, during a dispute about morning childcare support from their regular assistant, Priya declared that she would no longer fund this help from their joint household accounts. She insisted that having an assistant help with their son's morning routine was a personal luxury for Marcus alone—not a collective family necessity—and directed him to manage this expense separately using side income from his music gigs:

> **Priya (Jan 20, 2025):** *"El's not feeding our son in the morning and it doesn't seem to upset you. I will not be paying for El out of our earnings, pls manage cash separately... This is for you, not for the family."*

Marcus was trapped in a devastating economic and emotional bypass. He was carrying the daily physical operations of the household because Priya was physically unavailable due to her illness, yet the external support required to survive that workload was slandered as his personal convenience. His exhaustion was reframed as a private failure, his primary caregiving was minimized, and his side earnings were co-opted to fund what should have been a shared familial concern. He was expected to provide domestic backup, while his survival needs were treated as a personal tax outside the household's balance sheet.

## The Psychology

### Defensive Independence and the Dismissive-Avoidant Attachment System

In adult attachment literature (Mikulincer & Shaver, 2016), individuals with a *dismissive-avoidant* attachment style manage relationship anxiety through "compulsive self-reliance." 

When they feel vulnerable, threatened, or physically dependent on a partner (due to chronic illness, postpartum recovery, or emotional distress), their attachment system triggers a defensive reaction. They must convince themselves—and their partner—that they are completely independent and require no backup.

Priya's declaration—*"I will never need a man for it"*—is a textbook example of this defensive strategy. 

By declaring that she does not expect Marcus to put food on the table, she shields herself from the terrifying vulnerability of her actual situation: she is chronically ill, physically limited, and deeply dependent on her husband’s daily labor to keep her son safe and her household running. 

To acknowledge this dependency would be to grant Marcus equal standing and power in the relationship. By choosing to "never need a man," she keeps him in a state of permanent administrative subordination. He is allowed to provide the labor, but he is never allowed to hold the lease on her gratitude.

### Asymmetry in Relationship Power Structures

Research on marital power imbalances (Kolb & Williams, 2003) indicates that power is not just about who earns the money; it is about *asymmetry*. 

When one partner acts as the sole manager of all shared assets and accounts, while the other partner is relegated to an executional role, the relationship experiences a complete loss of equal footing. 

Priya’s consolidation of credit cards and reservations (*"From now on I will be primary"*) serves to establish an administrative monopoly. Marcus's contributions are absorbed into her system, but because she controls the keys, she can frame her management of those assets as a personal favor she is doing for him. This is what clinical systems call *benevolent control*: the controlling partner maintains dominance by framing their partner's dependency as evidence of their own generosity (*"I am going out of my way to pay your bills"*).

## The Damage Report

The damage caused by compiling a case for absolute independence is deep and systemic:

- **The Loss of Reciprocal Vulnerability:** Intimacy requires reciprocity. When one partner refuses to admit they need the other, the relationship loses its emotional density. The providing partner realizes they are funding a system in which their presence is treated as a temporary, replaceable convenience.
- **The Erosion of Trust:** Marcus is forced to live in a state of constant financial and physical exposure. He has no domestic safety net because he is constantly told that his partner has "no expectations" of him and would gladly replace him with her parents or nannies at any moment.
- **Structural Demoralization:** The partner who does the over-functioning is left with a profound sense of futility. They realize that no matter how much they sacrifice, how many diapers they change, or how much of their paycheck they surrender, they will never be logged as "necessary" in the eyes of their spouse.

---

## The Irony

The ultimate irony of this method is that the partner declaring complete independence is often the one who is most dependent on the system’s stability.

They look at themselves and say, *"I am a self-made, independent provider. I don’t need anyone."*

They fail to see that they are living in a house of cards that is entirely held up by the partner they claim to "never need."

Without Marcus's income flowing into her accounts, without Marcus's daily caregiving covering her physical crashes, without Marcus's compliance allowing her parents to live permanently in his space, Priya's "independent" empire would collapse in 24 hours. She has built her sovereignty on his submission, and then uses her sovereignty as proof that his submission was never required.
