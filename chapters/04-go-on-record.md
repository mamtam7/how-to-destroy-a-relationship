# Chapter 4: Go On Record
## *How to treat your private life as a trial exhibit*

---

> *"I will go on record to say I don't say that."*
> — Priya, January 4, 2025

---

## The Method

Before a relationship ends in a courtroom, it ends in the text thread.

The transition from a marriage to a legal dispute is rarely sudden. It is paved by a subtle, creeping shifting of the language two people use to communicate. In a healthy relationship, digital messages are messy, temporal, and direct. They are tools for immediate coordination and emotional check-ins. If there is an argument, it is sloppy and hot—an overflow of raw feeling that seeks immediate resolution.

But there is a method of communication that has a completely different purpose. It is the act of Going On Record.

When you use this method, you stop texting to speak to your partner. You start texting to speak to a future, third-party observer. You are no longer trying to solve an argument on a Tuesday afternoon; you are writing a transcript. You are drafting exhibits for a custody evaluator, a family court judge, a therapist, or an attorney.

This method operates through three distinct tactical patterns:

1. **The Legalistic Preface:** Actively using phrases like "to go on record" or "I will put this on record" inside domestic arguments. This serves as a warning shot to the partner: *this conversation is under surveillance.*
2. **The Weaponization of Therapy-Speak:** Using formal, clinical, or highly stylized psychological analyses to diagnose the partner's character flaws, family history, and communication skills. It wraps verbal hostility in the cool paper of a professional report.
3. **The Post-Conflict Delivery:** Sending highly structured, analytical paragraphs hours after a conflict has subsided or after the partner has de-escalated and apologized. Its function is to plant a permanent, uncontradicted monument of your narrative on the digital timeline.

The effect on the partner is a state of constant, low-level relational paranoia. They realize that they are no longer in a private space. They are being depositioned in their own home.

## In Practice

In early 2025, the language inside Marcus and Priya's text history underwent a striking transformation. The informal shorthand of their daily co-existence began to be replaced by formal, legalistic framing. 

On January 4, 2025, during an argument about household arrangements and parental visits, Priya invoked the record twice within an hour:

> **Priya (12:49 PM):** *"I am fine with your parents staying to go on record."*

> **Priya (1:21 PM):** *"I will go on record to say I don't say that."*

This is not the language of intimate negotiation. The phrase "to go on record" alters the nature of the text thread. It declares that the message is not just a spatial coordination (Marcus's parents' visit) but a legal ledger entry—a concessions ledger being balanced for a eventual future audit.

One week later, the tit-for-tat of household history was entered into the digital record using the same depositions frame:

> **Priya (Jan 11, 2025):** *"BTW to go on record, when James stayed with us for > 3 weeks, you didn't discuss with me either..."*

And on January 25, during a conflict about financial contributions and roles, Priya used the phrase to establish a unilateral, non-negotiable boundary:

> **Priya (Jan 25, 2025):** *"I will go on record – I don't expect you to put food on the table. I was raised to do it for myself and my family. I will never need a man for it."*

---

As the relationship progressed toward dissolution, the "on-record" framing escalated from basic legal phrasing to highly stylized, clinical accounts. 

On February 21, 2026, Marcus and Priya had a typical, painful argument about household coordination. Marcus, recognizing that the conflict was spiraling, attempted to withdraw and close the conversation:

> **Marcus (11:42 AM):** *"This conversation is over."*

The conflict stopped. Hours passed. Marcus assumed the situation was de-escalating. But at 2:47 PM, Priya delivered four consecutive, long paragraphs written in formal, highly clinical language that was completely inconsistent with the thousands of messages that had preceded it. 

These messages did not read like a wife speaking to her husband; they read like a psychiatric evaluation written by a professional evaluator:

> **Priya (2:47 PM):** *"I am writing to address how you handle interpersonal conflicts. It is important that you learn to manage your adult relationships in a kind, mature, and respectful manner..."*

> **Priya (2:47 PM):** *"It appears you lack a strong value system, operating instead from a place of control and a cycle of treating others as you have been treated. One can only pity this state of life."*

And regarding their son’s development:

> **Priya (2:51 PM):** *"I am writing to discuss our son's interpersonal development... I believe it is essential for him to take more personal responsibility for how he communicates and engages with others."*

And finally, the clinical diagnosis:

> **Priya (2:59 PM):** *"I am concerned that this pattern may stem from childhood trauma and may need to be reflected upon."*

This is the ultimate evolution of Going On Record. The messages use formal diagnostic phrases—*"clinical value system," "interpersonal development," "childhood trauma"*—not to seek mutual understanding or healing, but to create a permanent textual archive of Marcus's alleged psychological instability. It is a pre-litigation document delivered under the guise of an iMessage.

---

The pattern became so embedded in the household dynamic that it spilled over into spoken conversations. In later face-to-face arguments that were transcribed for legal clarity, Priya’s awareness of the recording process was explicit:

> **Priya:** *"You can go on record, feed it to AI... I don't need any thank you from anyone in life. Okay. How many times will I say that to you?"*

The virtual audience had fully entered the room. The "record" was no longer a silent consequence of texting; it was an active participant in their marriage.

## The Psychology

### Relational Surveillance and the Panopticon

In his classic work on power and surveillance, philosopher Michel Foucault described the *Panopticon*—a prison design where inmates never know when they are being watched, forcing them to assume they are under constant surveillance and thus police their own behavior at all times.

When a partner begins "going on record," they turn the relationship into a domestic Panopticon. 

The target partner realizes that any text message, any expression of frustration, any typo, or any late-night plea can be stored, summarized, and presented to an outside evaluator as evidence of their character. This destroys what psychologists call *relational safety*. Once it is gone, raw, honest, and vulnerable communication becomes impossible. 

The target must self-censor, drafting their messages with the same defensive legal care one would use when writing an email to a hostile employer. The marriage ceases to be an intimate sanctuary and becomes a workplace under hostile management.

### The Weaponization of "Therapy-Speak"

In recent years, sociologists and clinical psychologists have noted a rise in the weaponization of "therapy-speak"—the co-opting of terms designed for psychological healing (e.g., "boundaries," "gaslighting," "trauma," "interpersonal development") and using them to dominate or pathologize a partner.

This is what researchers term *manipulative pathologizing*. 

If Priya had texted Marcus, *"You are a terrible husband and your family is screwed up,"* the abuse would be obvious, and Marcus could easily dismiss it as her anger. But by writing, *"It appears you operate from a place of control... and this pattern may stem from childhood trauma,"* the hostility is masked by the authority of clinical science. It positions Priya as the objective, healthy clinician and Marcus as the damaged patient. It is a highly sophisticated form of invalidation because it makes the target’s natural defense look like a refusal to heal.

### The Pre-Litigation Posture and Relational Abandonment

From a systems perspective, when a partner begins "compiling a case," they have already psychologically abandoned the relationship. 

Relational repair requires vulnerability—which means leaving yourself open to being hurt. It requires admitting when you are wrong and hoping your partner handles your confession with care. 

But a legal case requires the opposite: you must never admit weakness, you must document every one of your partner's flaws, and you must frame every one of your own actions as pristine and beyond reproach. 

Thus, the moment a partner starts writing for "the record," they make repair impossible. They are no longer maintaining the marriage; they are managing its liquidation.

## The Damage Report

The damage caused by the Go On Record method is severe and irreparable:

- **The death of conversational intimacy:** Text messages can no longer be used for sloppy, raw, or playful connection. Every interaction must be polished and defensive.
- **The destruction of epistemic safety:** The target partner stops talking because they know that any text they send can and will be used against them in a future dispute. They retreat into absolute digital silence.
- **The flattening of history:** Messy, complex, multi-year human lives are flattened into a selective ledger of trial exhibits. The narrative of the relationship is stripped of its love, joy, and mutual care, leaving only a clinical record of conflict.

---

## The Irony

The irony of this method is that the partner going on record believes they are demonstrating their own maturity and self-awareness. 

They write these long, clinical paragraphs hours after a fight and tell themselves, *"Look how calm I am. Look how professional I am. I am not shouting; I am diagnosing."* They believe they are laying down a record of their own sanity and patience.

They do not see that the act of writing a brief for a future judge is the ultimate act of betrayal. 

They do not see that by treating their spouse as a subject in a clinical case file, they are actively executing the very relationship they claim to be trying to save. They are so busy preparing a perfect post-mortem report that they are suffocating the living person in front of them.
